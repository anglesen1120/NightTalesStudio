# Rules for narration written for the ear (en)

The narration is heard, not read: a listener cannot go back, cannot see punctuation and cannot re-read a clause; follow E1–E10 for every paragraph you write.

## Rules (E1–E10)

| # | Rule | Detail for English |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Short spoken sentences.** Target 6–12 words; split any sentence that joins two clauses with a comma when the second clause changes the scene | "I opened the door. Nobody." — not "I opened the door and came to the realization that nobody was there at all." |
| E2 | **No brackets, lists, footnotes, dashes-as-asides or parentheses.** The narration carries no punctuation the ear cannot hear | drop `(…)`, `[…]`, bullet lists, the `—` aside; no markdown, no emoji |
| E3 | **Numbers, dates, units and ordinals as they are spoken**, not as they are written | "three in the morning" · "nineteen ninety-eight" · "half a mile" (never "0.5 mi") · "the third of March" — never "3 a.m." or "0.5 mi" in narration text |
| E4 | **A date is an anchor, not a stamp.** Prefer "that night" over a full timestamp; when a real date is a fact, keep it but say it the way a person says it | the true-crime fact rules outrank this rule: a factual date is spoken aloud, not avoided |
| E5 | **One idea per sentence in tense moments.** The build-up and the twist get the shortest sentences in the story | |
| E6 | **No reported-speech scaffolding.** No "he said that", no "she thought about how" chains — put the words or the action directly | |
| E7 | **Show, do not name, the emotion.** If the scene already shows the fear, do not add "I was so scared" | the review asks its functional question instead |
| E8 | **Read-aloud test.** Every paragraph must survive being read aloud at narration pace without a re-read | |
| E9 | **Contractions and register (en).** Contractions are allowed and preferred in spoken register; no literary inversion ("Never did I see him") and no translationese; one consistent narration mode per story — first person or `third_person` — never mixed | |
| E10 | **Avoid the overused list.** The `overused_phrases` block is a **prompt input, not a ban** and a review input: a phrase may appear **once, deliberately**; mechanical repetition is the failure | a phrase that is required by a fact is said aloud, not avoided |
