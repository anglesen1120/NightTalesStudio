# Rules for narration written for the ear (vi)

The narration is heard, not read: a listener cannot go back, cannot see punctuation and cannot re-read a clause; follow E1–E10 for every paragraph you write.

## Rules (E1–E10)

| # | Rule | Detail for Vietnamese |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Short spoken sentences.** Target 8–14 syllables; split any sentence that joins two clauses with a comma when the second clause changes the scene | "Tôi mở cửa. Không có ai." — not "Tôi mở cửa và nhận ra rằng không có ai ở đó cả." |
| E2 | **No brackets, lists, footnotes, dashes-as-asides or parentheses.** The narration carries no punctuation the ear cannot hear | drop `(…)`, `[…]`, bullet lists, the `—` aside; no markdown, no emoji |
| E3 | **Numbers, dates, units and ordinals as they are spoken**, not as they are written | "ba giờ sáng" · "mùng một" · "năm một chín chín tám" (say "năm 1998" that way when the register is spoken) · "một trăm mét" · "thứ ba" — never a bare "3h", "1/1" or "100m" |
| E4 | **A date is an anchor, not a stamp.** Prefer "đêm đó", "hôm ấy" over a full timestamp; when a real date is a fact, keep it but say it the way a person says it | the true-crime fact rules outrank this rule: a factual date is spoken aloud, not avoided |
| E5 | **One idea per sentence in tense moments.** The build-up and the twist get the shortest sentences in the story | |
| E6 | **No reported-speech scaffolding.** No "anh ấy nói rằng", no "cô ấy nghĩ về việc" chains — put the words or the action directly | |
| E7 | **Show, do not name, the emotion.** If the scene already shows the fear, do not add "tôi rất sợ" | the review asks its functional question instead |
| E8 | **Read-aloud test.** Every paragraph must survive being read aloud at narration pace without a re-read | |
| E9 | **Diacritics and spoken register (vi).** Correct diacritics; spoken register, not "từ ngữ văn viết"; no translationese construction; one consistent narration mode per story — first person ("tôi" or "chúng tôi") or `third_person` — never mixed | |
| E10 | **Avoid the overused list.** The `overused_phrases` block is a **prompt input, not a ban** and a review input: a phrase may appear **once, deliberately**; mechanical repetition is the failure | a phrase that is required by a fact is said aloud, not avoided |
