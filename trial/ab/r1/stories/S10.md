The Light Remembers

I kept the lighthouse on a cliff where the waves faded before dawn. The lamp turned itself on three nights after I pulled its fuse.

I took the keeper's post in late autumn. The lighthouse stood beyond a narrow road, with nothing around it but rock, grass, and black water. The tower was old, but the lamp had been replaced years before. Its power ran through a fuse box inside the keeper's room.

The previous keeper left one instruction behind. He wrote it inside the first page of the log. Record every time the lamp starts without power. Do not assume the switch was left on. I thought it was an odd way to begin a job.

The first night passed without trouble. Wind pressed against the tower windows. Water tapped the glass in small, regular beats. Near midnight, I heard the lantern turn above me. The sound came through the ceiling as a slow mechanical hum.

I climbed the stairs with a torch in my hand. The lamp was burning behind its thick glass. Its beam moved across the sea, though the fuse had not been touched. I pulled the fuse again and waited until the light died.

The next morning, I checked every wire. Nothing looked damaged. The switch sat in the off position. A thin line of moisture ran beneath it, as though water had gathered on the wall. I wiped it away and found the paint cold beneath my fingers.

I wrote the event into the log before breakfast. I used the same plain words as the instruction. Lamp active after fuse removal. Weather calm. No vessel offshore. Then I closed the book and placed a metal cup on top of it.

The cup was on the floor when I returned. The log lay open beneath the window. My entry had not changed. Below it, a new line had appeared. It contained only a time and three words. The light remembers the way.

I searched the older pages that afternoon. The same sentence appeared several times, written in different years. The dates were separated by decades. Each entry described the lamp starting after the fuse was pulled. None mentioned who had written the sentence.

That night, I heard footsteps below the room. They came slowly up the spiral stairs. Each step creaked once. Then the sound stopped outside my door. I opened it after a minute. The landing was empty, but the stone floor was wet.

The rain had not reached that side of the tower. I checked the stairs and found a trail of damp marks leading upward. They ended at the lantern room. At the top, the lamp was dark. The fuse sat in my palm, warm from the box.

I opened the log before going downstairs. A new entry filled the next page. The writing looked older than mine, with narrow letters and heavy pressure. It described the footsteps I had just heard. At the bottom, it named the hour when the lamp would return.

The lamp came on at that hour. I was standing beside the fuse box. I watched the switch move down by itself. The beam crossed the sea, and the sound of waves below began to fade. It did not grow quieter with distance. It seemed to withdraw from the tower.

I ran back to the keeper's room. The last page was no longer blank. It held a full account of the night. The words described my hands on the fuse box, my wet coat, and the cup on the floor. The handwriting was not mine. It was the writing from the oldest entries.

At the bottom of the page, one sentence waited alone. The keeper always leaves before the sea goes silent. Behind me, the stairs gave one slow creak. The door to the lantern room opened, though I had locked it.

I did not climb after it. I took my coat and went outside. The wind had stopped along the cliff. Far below, the water moved without making a sound. The tower lamp turned across the empty horizon.

I walked to the road and kept going. Behind me, the light continued its slow sweep. It passed over the wet grass, then the road, then the place where I stood. Each time it reached me, the waves vanished completely.

At the village, I handed the keys to the harbor office. I said the tower was unsafe. I did not mention the log. When they asked about the last entry, I told them I had not written it. That answer made the room go quiet.

The lighthouse stayed closed through the winter. No one found damage in the wiring. The fuse box was removed. The lamp still appeared twice, according to the boats offshore. Both crews reported the same thing. The beam crossed the water while the waves made no sound.

The log was returned to me in spring. Its last page remained filled. I keep it in a drawer beneath my bed. I have never copied the words. I have never thrown it away. On damp evenings, the drawer smells faintly of salt.

By morning, the lamp was dark again. The sea had gone quiet below the rocks. I left the log open on the table, with the last page turned down. I have not returned to the lighthouse since. Still, on wet nights, I hear waves fading behind my house. Beneath them comes the soft click of a switch. Have you ever heard something like this?
