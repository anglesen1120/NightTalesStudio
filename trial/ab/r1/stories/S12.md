The Chair Above My Ceiling

I was the last tenant at the end of the corridor. Every night, a chair dragged above my ceiling. There was only a roof terrace up there.

I rented the flat during a wet summer. The building had six floors and one narrow stairwell. My room sat beneath the roof access. Above me was a locked terrace, used for nothing. The paint inside the building had gone soft from the humidity. It smelled old even after the windows were open.

The first sound came on my third night. It began after midnight. Something scraped across the ceiling above my bed. The noise moved slowly, like wooden legs crossing concrete. It stopped near the far wall. I waited for another sound. Nothing came. Then the scrape began again.

I asked the tenant across from me about it. He listened without looking up from his keys. He said the roof terrace had been empty for years. The access door stayed locked because the latch was broken. He had never heard anything above his flat. Then he shut his door.

The chair dragged at the same hour for four nights. I began leaving the lamp on. The sound always started above the bed. It crossed the ceiling in three long movements. After that, I heard a small pause. The final scrape stopped directly above my pillow.

The smell changed on the fifth night. Old paint filled the room, thick and damp. I found pale flakes on the floor beside my bed. They were not from my ceiling. The plaster above me looked whole. I swept the flakes into a cup and left it by the window.

The next evening, I checked the stairwell. The roof door stood above the sixth floor. Its paint was blistered and yellow. A chain passed through the handle. I touched it once. The metal felt warm, though the stairwell was cooler than my room.

The chair became easier to picture after that. It sounded wooden. One leg caught on the floor each time it moved. I imagined it crossing the terrace in darkness. The thought stayed with me while I worked. At night, I kept my shoes beside the bed.

On the seventh night, the scrape started earlier. It came before the building grew quiet. I went upstairs and stood beside the roof door. The chain trembled on the handle. No wind reached that stairwell. From behind the door came one slow drag.

I noticed marks on the wall beside the door. They were low and horizontal. The paint had been rubbed away in three places. The marks pointed upward, toward the terrace. I placed my hand over one of them. It was damp, and it smelled like the flakes from my room.

The tenant across the hall found me there. He looked at the door and went pale. He told me the chair had belonged to a former caretaker. Years earlier, it had been kept on the roof. He said it was usually tied to the railing before anyone went up.

He also told me something I had not asked. The caretaker used to drag the chair indoors during storms. The chair had one short leg. You could hear it catch on the floor. He had heard that sound from below, long after the caretaker left the building.

That night, the scraping stopped above my pillow. I heard a second sound instead. It came from the corridor outside my door. A wooden leg touched the floor. Then another. The movement ended at my threshold. The handle did not turn.

I went to the roof before dawn. The chain was still around the handle, but it hung loose. The door stood open by several inches. It had opened inward, against the direction of the wind. Old paint smell drifted through the gap.

The terrace was empty at first glance. Then I saw the chair beside the far parapet. It faced the open doorway. One leg rested on a patch of fresh paint. The chair was wet, though the terrace was dry. Behind me, the door moved wider.

A scrape sounded from the middle of the terrace. The chair shifted one short distance toward me. Its damaged leg caught on the concrete. Nothing touched its back. I stepped through the doorway, and the chair stopped.

The door swung shut behind me. The latch clicked from the stairwell side. Across the terrace, the chair faced the place where I stood. Below my feet, another scrape began. It was coming from my room.

I did not cross the terrace. I waited until daylight reached the parapet. The chair remained where it was. When I opened the door again, the chain was hanging in place. The chair was no longer beside the parapet.

It stood outside my flat. The seat faced my door. Pale paint flakes covered the floor around it. I checked every room before touching the handle. Nothing had been disturbed. The chair left no marks on the corridor tiles.

After that morning, the sound changed. It no longer came from above. At midnight, I heard the chair move along the corridor. It stopped outside my door each night. I never opened it. In the morning, it always stood a little closer to the stairs.

I moved out before the next rent was due. The corridor stayed quiet while I carried my boxes downstairs. On my last night, I looked back from the entrance. The chair stood at the far end, beneath the peeling light. It faced the stairwell. I still do not know who dragged it there. Have you ever heard something like this?
