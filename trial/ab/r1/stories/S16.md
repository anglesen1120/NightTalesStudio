The Bottle Left at the Shrine

The bottle was full when I returned to the shrine. I had left it empty three trips before. Small footprints crossed the dust beside it.

I drove long routes between industrial towns and mountain passes. That road had few lights after sunset. I stopped beside the shrine because my eyes needed rest.

It was hardly more than a roof over a stone shelf. A narrow lamp sat inside. The flame had gone out, but the wick still smelled of smoke.

I had one bottle left in the cab. I placed it beneath the shelf. The night was cool enough to keep the water clear.

Before I left, I heard a dry voice near the trees. It asked for water on the next trip. I looked around, but saw only branches moving above the road.

I answered without thinking. I said I would bring another bottle. Then I climbed back into the cab and drove until dawn.

The next morning, I remembered the bottle. I pictured it sitting under the roof. I also remembered the promise, though I could not remember who had asked.

On my return, I brought two bottles. One was for me. One was for the voice. I placed the second beneath the shelf and accepted the same duty again.

The road kept drawing me past that place. Every trip ended near the same hour. I began carrying an extra bottle before I reached the hills.

I never saw anyone waiting there. Still, the crickets stopped whenever I opened the cab door. Their silence lasted until the engine started.

The third time, I noticed prints beside the shelf. They were small and bare. They began at the water and ended at the edge of the road.

I followed them for a few steps. They faded where the ground turned hard. No grass was bent. No stone had shifted.

An old brass cup rested behind the bottle. I had not seen it before. A dark line circled its rim, as though someone had filled it many times.

On the next trip, I found my bottle full again. The cap was still sealed. My own bottle was empty in the cab.

The prints surrounded the shelf. They crossed over mine and returned toward the trees. The voice came from there again, softer than before.

It asked why I had stopped bringing the water I owed. I said I had brought it every time. The crickets began calling from much closer.

In the cab mirror, the shrine stood behind me. A second shape occupied the doorway. I saw only a small hand resting against the frame.

The lamp inside lit by itself. The bottle on the shelf tipped toward the doorway. The footprints led away from it, toward the road I had just traveled.

The voice named the debt then. Years earlier, another driver had promised water before leaving that road. He never returned. I was paying what he had left behind.

I stayed until morning. I did not sleep. At first light, I placed every bottle from the cab beneath the shelf.

The small prints appeared once more. They crossed the clearing and stopped at the road. After that, they never came back.

The brass cup disappeared with them. Only a pale ring remained on the stone. I wiped it away before starting the engine.

For several months, I avoided that route. The longer roads cost more fuel. They also left me with the sound of crickets in every rest stop.

Then one evening, I found a full bottle on the passenger seat. I had locked the cab. The seal was unbroken, and dust covered the cap.

I still stop there when the road takes me past. I bring water before I bring anything else. The dust stays smooth now. The bottle stays empty. I never learned whose thirst I answered, only that the promise is settled. What would you have done?
