The earliest account came from a station notebook. The clerk who took it still holds the original, according to the record. That first version differs from the court version in its wording and in its length.

Before the trial, the accused withdrew the first statement. The record says he objected to its wording. He said it did not read the way he had spoken.

That objection gave the earlier version a new importance. The station statement was not merely shorter or longer. According to the record, its wording changed as well. The accused challenged that change before the case reached court.

The version heard by the court carried the weight of the prosecution. It was read aloud as the statement on which the conviction rested, according to the later finding. The room heard one account as though it were settled.

Elsewhere, the clerk kept his private notebook from that year. The record says it remains in his possession. Its contents match the station version, not the version read in court.

That match is the first fixed point in the surviving record. The clerk's notebook agrees with the statement taken at the station. It does not agree with the court version.

The notebook runs without gaps, according to the record. No missing pages are noted. No interruption appears in the account of the writing that survives.

The same record says the notebook was never entered as evidence in the original proceedings. It stayed with the clerk instead. The version outside the courtroom was preserved privately.
