The Blackboard in Classroom Seven

I was the only person inside the old school that night. From classroom seven came the soft scrape of chalk. The room had been locked for years.

I had worked as the school's caretaker for almost six years. The building closed after the evening shift disappeared. Most rooms stayed locked. I checked the halls after sunset, emptied the bins, and shut off the lights. Classroom seven was always last.

The door had swollen in its frame. I needed both hands to open it. Dust covered the floor in an even grey layer. Nothing had crossed it since the previous winter. At least, nothing that left footprints.

The first time I heard chalk, I blamed the heating pipes. They clicked inside the walls as the temperature fell. Still, the sound came in a steady rhythm. Scratch. Pause. Scratch. It stopped whenever I reached the corridor outside.

The corridor light above the room had begun to flicker. It stayed dark for three seconds, then returned with a weak yellow glow. Each time it came back, the classroom door seemed closer. I measured the distance once. I stopped doing that after.

On the fourth evening, I entered with a lantern. The room smelled of dust and damp plaster. Desks stood in their old rows. A wooden ruler lay beside the teacher's desk, covered in a film of grey powder.

The blackboard was not clean. A surname filled the center of it in tall, narrow letters. I knew the handwriting from old staff records. The evening-shift teacher had used that same careful script.

The teacher vanished during the school's final year. Nobody saw him leave. His coat remained in the staff room, and his bicycle stayed chained outside. People stopped using the word missing after a while. They called him gone.

I wiped the surname away with a damp cloth. White dust gathered in the grooves of the board. When I finished, the cloth was dry. The chalk ledge beneath it was wet enough to leave a shining mark.

I checked the windows. They were painted shut from the inside. The ceiling hatch was sealed with rusted screws. There was no second door. I locked the classroom, then stood in the corridor until the light steadied.

The next morning, I searched the staff records. His final lesson plan was still filed beneath the old timetable. The last line had been crossed out. Beside it, in the margin, he had written, Leave the board until the bell.

There was no bell in the building anymore. The main clock had stopped during the closure. Yet classroom seven had its own small clock above the board. Its hands pointed to the same hour every night.

I went back after dusk and watched from the doorway. The clock remained still. The corridor light blinked once. Then the chalk began moving across the board, though no hand held it.

It wrote the teacher's surname again. The letters appeared slowly, one stroke at a time. I could hear the chalk touch the board. I could also hear breathing near the windows. The windows stayed closed.

At the bottom of the board, a second line appeared. It was not a name. It was the hour written in words. That was the hour when the teacher had vanished. I had seen it in the old report.

The chalk stopped beneath the second line. For several seconds, nothing moved. Then a new word appeared below it. Mine. Not my surname. The name my mother had used before I entered school.

I had never written that name in the caretaker's office. It was not on my records. Only the teacher had known it. He used it once when I started the job, then never spoke to me again.

The classroom clock began to tick. The hands moved backward toward the hour of the disappearance. Behind me, the locked door opened by itself. On the board, the teacher's surname shifted lower, making space above it.

A final line formed at the top. It said, You were absent yesterday. From inside the empty room came the scrape of a chair being pulled across the floor.

I left the lantern on the floor and walked out. I did not run. The corridor light followed me in its broken rhythm. Behind each dark doorway, something made the small sound of chalk being set down.

At the gate, I found white dust on my coat. It covered the sleeves and shoulders. The wind had been blowing toward the school all night. The dust had settled on me from behind.

I returned after sunrise with new locks. Classroom seven was shut. The key turned once, then stopped. From inside came three quiet taps against the door. They followed the rhythm of a lesson beginning.

Since then, the school has stayed empty. I still pass it sometimes before dawn. The windows are black, except for classroom seven. Behind that glass, a pale line appears, then fades before I can read it.

I never wiped that board again. The next morning, the classroom door stood open. Every other door in the corridor was locked. Inside, the blackboard was clean except for one wet line beneath the teacher's name. It read, Stay until the lesson ends. I left the school before sunrise. The corridor light flickered behind me, one room at a time. Have you ever heard something like this?
