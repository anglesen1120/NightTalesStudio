The Chair Above My Ceiling

I lived at the end of a narrow corridor. Every night, a chair dragged across the ceiling above me. There was only a locked roof terrace up there.

The building was old enough to hold heat after sunset. Paint lifted in thin curls along the walls. My room sat beneath the roof access. Above it was a flat terrace, empty except for vents and a rusted drain.

The first sound came during my second week. It began with one scrape near the window. Then came three shorter scrapes toward the door. The noise stopped there. I blamed loose furniture in another apartment.

The next night, it came at the same hour. I was lying in bed with the window open. Humid air pressed against the curtains. Underneath it, I could smell old paint, warm plaster, and something dusty.

I asked the tenant across the hall about it. He listened without looking at the ceiling. Then he told me the floor above had no rooms. He said the roof door had been locked since before he moved in.

That evening, I checked the stairwell. The roof door had a chain looped through its handle. Dust covered the threshold. Nothing marked it except a pale line where the door had rubbed the floor.

The scraping returned after midnight. It sounded closer than before. I heard the chair cross the ceiling, pause, and move back again. Each pass ended directly above my bed. The old paint smell filled the room.

I slept in the sitting room for three nights. The sound followed me there. It crossed above the sofa instead. The pattern stayed the same. One long drag, three short ones, then silence.

The landlord sent a maintenance worker to inspect the ceiling. He found no pipes, loose boards, or animals. Before leaving, he glanced toward the stairwell. He asked whether the noise had begun after I moved in.

I asked what he meant. He only said the last tenant had complained about furniture moving overhead. That tenant had left without collecting his deposit. The worker refused to say anything else.

The following night, I placed a glass on the bedroom floor. I wanted to hear whether the scraping came through the room or the ceiling. At midnight, the glass trembled before the first sound began.

The chair dragged from the window side again. This time, I heard a faint knock beneath the scrape. It came three times. The knocks matched the sound I made when testing the roof door.

I went upstairs and stood below the roof access. The chain was still in place. I touched the handle. Above me, something dragged once across the terrace. Then the chain gave a soft metallic rattle.

The worker returned the next morning. He saw the pale line beneath the door and went quiet. He told me never to open it after midnight. When I asked why, he said the terrace had never been empty.

I asked what was there. He rubbed paint dust from his fingers. Years earlier, someone had carried a chair upstairs during a heat wave. Nobody remembered bringing it back down. After that, tenants heard it moving.

The story sounded ridiculous in daylight. Yet the marks beneath the door looked fresh. Four narrow tracks crossed the dust inside the threshold. They pointed outward, toward the stairwell, not inward toward the terrace.

That night, the scraping began earlier. It crossed above my room, then stopped. I waited with my shoes on. The old paint smell thickened around me. From the ceiling came one careful knock.

I picked up my keys and climbed. The corridor behind me was empty. At the top landing, the chain hung loose. The roof door stood open by the width of a hand.

The door had opened from the inside. I could see the chain lying beyond the threshold. Warm air moved through the gap, carrying the smell of old paint. Something scraped across the terrace.

The terrace held one wooden chair. It faced the open doorway. As I watched, its legs shifted across the concrete and stopped just inside the light.

I did not step onto the terrace. The chair remained still for several seconds. Then it turned toward the stairwell without lifting from the ground. The scraping sound traveled down through the building.

I ran back to my room and locked the door. The sound stopped above me. A moment later, the chair dragged across the corridor outside my apartment. It stopped on the other side of my door.

There was no knock. I watched the handle until morning. At sunrise, I opened the door. Four thin tracks crossed the dust outside, leading toward the stairs.

The roof door was closed again. The chain hung through its handle. On the terrace, the chair was gone. I never learned whether it had returned upstairs, or whether it had only been waiting outside.

I moved out before the month was over. The new tenant asked why I left. I told him the ceiling carried sound from the roof. He laughed, then asked if I had heard the chair. I had not told him about the chair. Have you ever heard something like this?
