The house at the bend of the lane had stood empty for years, yet every November its windows glowed faintly at dusk. Mara the weaver swore she saw a woman in grey hanging linen in the front room, folding each sheet twice before laying it down. No one believed her until the night the miller's boy chased his stray dog through the open door and came out pale. He said the sheets smelled of river water and someone had hummed his name from the cellar. After that the villagers kept their dogs close and their lanterns lit until well past morning. When the roof finally fell in, the sound it made was soft and almost relieved, like a long breath let out at last. For weeks afterward, children dared each other to peer through the fallen rafters, swearing they saw folded linen stacked neatly beneath the rubble where none had been left. Mara walked past the ruin every evening on her way home, never once looking directly at it, though she always slowed her step near the old wooden gate.
